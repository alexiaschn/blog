# An imperfect AI to uphold human agency : vindicating the 'yes but' of this world

This presentation addresses the potential for a renewed agentive contract from researchers motivated by a simple yet very potent impulse to correct in relation to current AI assistant in information retrieval systems. 

The persuasiveness of perceived objectivity in computers' outputs [@dijkstraPersuasivenessExpertSystems1998] and algorithmic appreciation despite blindness to an algorithm’s process [@loggAlgorithmAppreciationPeople2019] are not novel biases. What is new is the increased presence of automated decision-making tools in all aspects of our life and the yet unidentified impact of AI on individual agency even after the AI tools are removed from the decision process [@vicenteHumansInheritArtificial2023]. Chatbots are being relied upon for a variety of reasons that still need to be fullly grappled with, such as a perceived lack of expertise and confidence, or a penchant for hedonism [@bookbinderWhyAIDestroying2025], encouraging AIs' sycophancy and the invisibilisation of their use in what we put under our name, while reinforcing the authoritative role of those machines. 

On the other hand, rising concerns over cognitive offloading [@kosmynaYourBrainChatGPT2025] is also renewing interest in teaching critical thinkings skills as part of a broader digital literacy including AI [@goodladEditorsIntroductionHumanities2023]. Possible avenues for mitigating cognitive offloading have been explored in the form of structured prompting and reflective engagement [@gerlichOffloadingEngagementExperimental2025]. Another area of collaborative reflective use of generative AI is in teaching, with for instance, a specialized prompting strategy allowing a chatbot to become a teacher tailored to the needs of a spectific taught skill [@abditabariComparingEffectsTeacher2025; @jinalpurohitFeedbackLearnerMotivation2025]. However, little has been done to assert the user as a potential teacher in their interactions with generative AI tools and leverage corrective feedbacks from the user to foster engagement and critical thinking. From this tension between letting the machine shape our ideas and the desire to correct and begin a dialog emerges the opportunity to shape new practices in research and pedagogy.  

The presentation will rely on the close examination of a small scale user-study conducted on an application designed to help a user co-construct queries sent to a scientific search engine. The application initially relied on the limited availability of pre-translated terms into a language meant as a semantic decompositional matrix in 9 ubiquitous facets. Later, an 'automatic translation' feature was added, which relied on a prompted LLM with few-shots. Since the translations generated were of uneven quality by all measure, users were given the possibility to correct and validate the translation. During this process, all users reacted and debated the proposed translation, even going as far as using the text areas to provide direct feedback. This presentation challenges the idea that a tool's legitimacy relies on its accuracy and defends that the dysfunctional [@vitali-rosatiElogeBugEtre2024] can be a fertile component of collaborative dialogical process between imperfect algorithms and the observed human tendency to correct.
